## 3. HTTP upload: it works now, and why it failed before

Tested with a raw-socket `POST /rr_upload?name=&time=&crc32=` carrying
`X-Session-Key` from `rr_connect` (scripts and logs in
`runs/wifi_2026-10-28/`):

- 13 of 13 uploads succeeded, 4 kB to 1.4 MB, verified by `rr_fileinfo`.
  1.4 MB took 31 to 37 s (about 40 kB/s); 300 kB took 6 to 28 s.
- Sends stall in steps of 0.3, 0.6, 1.2, 2.4 s: the Windows TCP
  retransmission back-off. Each stall is a burst of lost frames. RRF closes
  an HTTP session idle for 8 s, so a few consecutive losses push the
  back-off past 8 s and the Duet drops the connection with nothing written.
  That is the "fails at 8 s" of the 27th, and of one test right after the
  flash. With the better signal the stalls stay short.
- Duet Web Control uses the same request with the same odds per attempt,
  plus up to two automatic retries (`ajaxRetries` in dwc-settings.json),
  which is why it "just works".
- `Connection: keep-alive` is required to get the `200 OK` back; with
  `Connection: close` the Duet writes the file but sends no status line.
- Pacing the sends (small SO_SNDBUF, small writes) did not help consistently.

Consequences for `duet.py` and the README:

- USB `M28` stays the primary upload (lossless, 150 kB/s, four times faster).
- The cable-less fallback should become `rr_upload` with retries and
  keep-alive, verified by `rr_fileinfo` size, instead of the 8 kB chunk files
  and their pause caveat. `upload_wifi_chunked` can go once that is in.
- The README's link table, the "any request bigger than a few hundred bytes
  stalls" paragraph, and the "web interface unreliable" status line are out of
  date. `M122` over Wi-Fi (`rr_gcode` then `rr_reply`) worked fine today; the
  "commands never sent" list was about USB and the 22:05 crash, keep it for
  USB.

## 5. Add this: a Blender render of what the print will look like

The owner wants a quick-and-dirty **BlenderReality** render of the sliced part
in the approval step, alongside the OpenSCAD preview. Not pretty, just a
picture of the print as the toolpath would build it: layer lines, overhangs,
the first layer's footprint.

- **BlenderReality is its own git repo at `D:\Source\BlenderReality`** (not
  part of SymbolicTM; SymbolicTM only has a client of it in
  `src/symbolictm/handlers/`). Read its `README.md` and `documents/HOUSE_RULES.md`
  (one level up, in the Turing Mill monorepo's `documents/`).
- Shape: `worker.py` runs under Blender's own Python, headless
  (`blender -b --factory-startup -P worker.py -- job.json`); `client.py` is
  bpy-free and importable from any env; `protocol/` has the verbs
  `reset_scene`, `deposit`, `mill`, `capture`, `capture_range`, `snapshot`, and
  `protocol/gcode.py` parses printer G-code (G0/G1, G90/G91, M82/M83, G92;
  tool changes and temperatures ignored) into extruding polylines.
- `deposit(name, symbol_gcode=PATH, bead_radius=0.2)` turns the extruding
  moves of a `.gcode` file into bead tubes (a curve with a circular bevel plus
  noise, a BUILD modifier so it grows layer by layer). That is the "reality"
  render: feed it the agent's `NAME.T2.gcode`, then `snapshot` or `capture`
  from a wide position. Each `run()` is one Blender process consuming one job;
  start every job with `reset_scene`.
- It was built for 5 mm symbols. A 1.4 MB part G-code is tens of thousands of
  polylines; expect the bead mesh to be heavy. Quick-and-dirty levers: low
  resolution and few samples on the capture, a larger `bead_radius`, and
  subsampling the layers (every nth layer, or the first layer plus the
  outline) before handing the G-code over. If even that is too slow, the
  honest fallback is a small new verb in the worker that imports the STL as
  a mesh (Blender's STL importer) for a plain shaded shot. That is a change in
  the BlenderReality repo, with its own commit and test, and `bpy` stays in
  `worker.py` only.
- Blender 2.92, 3.6 and 4.3 are installed under `C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\`;
  the client finds one through `BLENDER_REALITY_EXE`, PATH or the standard
  install folders. SymbolicTM pins 4.3.2 for its science; the part render has
  no such pin, but use 4.3 so one worker serves both.
- Where it sits in the plan: phase 1 gains `part.py render-blender NAME.T2.gcode`
  producing `NAME.blender.png`; phase 2's skill shows both pictures, the
  OpenSCAD geometry and the Blender build, before asking to print; phase 3's
  dry run includes it. Measure how long it takes on the ball joint and record
  the number in the plan.
